# Marketing Case Playbook

Use for take-home assignments, interviews, consulting exercises, and other time-boxed marketing cases.

## Time-Box the Work

For a four-to-six-hour case, use an approximate allocation:

- 10%: Decision framing, product context, and research plan.
- 25%: Market, competitor, and customer evidence.
- 15%: Partner, creator, or channel evaluation.
- 15%: Message, offer, landing-page, or campaign concept.
- 15%: Measurement, experimentation, and execution plan.
- 15%: Deck and supporting spreadsheet.
- 5%: Source, consistency, and visual QA.

Adjust by evaluation criteria. Stop research when additional facts no longer change the recommendation.

## Recommended Artifact Architecture

Use a concise main deck and an auditable supporting workbook.

### Main deck

1. Executive recommendation and assumptions.
2. Product and decision context.
3. Market or competitive landscape.
4. Decision-relevant findings and whitespace.
5. Target customer and research plan.
6. Channel, creator, or partner priorities.
7. Landing page, offer, or activation concept.
8. Measurement and learning loop.
9. First two weeks or 30/60/90-day execution plan.
10. AI workflow, limitations, and next evidence.

Use fewer slides when possible. Each slide needs a takeaway headline, evidence, and implication.

### Supporting workbook

- Competitor matrix with normalized prices and sources.
- Customer research instrument and response coding plan.
- Creator or partner longlist, scoring model, and source links.
- Claim ledger and source log.
- KPI dictionary, event plan, and experiment backlog.

## Evaluation Logic

Demonstrate:

- ownership by making reasonable assumptions explicit and moving forward
- research quality through source choice and comparable fields
- structured thinking through a decision framework, not volume
- judgment through prioritization, exclusions, and trade-offs
- AI capability through corrected before-and-after examples
- execution through owners, timing, dependencies, and first actions
- measurement through decision-linked metrics and a learning loop
- communication through answer-first slides and concise appendices

## Recommendation Standard

A recommendation should state:

- what to do
- which segment or use case to prioritize
- why the evidence supports it
- what not to prioritize yet
- the main risk or dependency
- what test should happen next
- what result would change the recommendation

## Final QA

- Recheck all live prices and links on the submission date.
- Ensure slide claims match workbook evidence.
- Ensure the survey tests hypotheses rather than advertising the answer.
- Ensure creator scores are based on fit and actionability, not follower count alone.
- Ensure the landing-page CTA produces a measurable signal stronger than a generic visit.
- Ensure the data loop explains how findings change messaging, targeting, pricing, or product decisions.
- Remove unsupported superlatives and medical claims.
- Keep AI-generated prose from sounding generic or repetitive.
