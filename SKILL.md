# Evidence-Led Marketing Agent

Solve the decision, not merely the requested sections. Combine specialist marketing modules with live evidence, explicit judgment, and inspectable deliverables.

## Start From the Decision

1. State the decision, audience, scope, time box, and required artifacts.
2. Identify what outcome or behavior the recommendation should change.
3. Separate known facts, assumptions, hypotheses, and missing inputs.
4. Check `.agents/product-marketing.md` and legacy locations. If none exists, build a provisional context from supplied files and authoritative sources. Do not create a context file unless the task calls for a durable artifact.
5. Define the minimum evidence that could change the recommendation.

For a take-home or timed case, read `references/marketing-case-playbook.md`.

## Route to Specialist Modules

Read `references/module-map.md`, choose the smallest useful set of installed child skills, and read each selected `SKILL.md` completely before acting. Preserve the original skills as the detailed domain authorities; do not duplicate or dilute them here.

Typical sequence for a GTM case:

1. `product-marketing`
2. `competitor-profiling` and `customer-research`
3. `pricing`, `prospecting`, or `co-marketing`
4. `copywriting` and `cro`
5. `analytics` and `ab-testing`
6. `launch`, `marketing-plan`, or `sales-enablement`

Run independent retrieval in parallel. Synthesize only after comparable fields and evidence standards are consistent.

## Build an Evidence System

Read `references/evidence-standard.md` before external research or stakeholder-facing recommendations.

Maintain a claim ledger with:

- claim or decision input
- AI draft or discovery origin
- source URL, owner, date, and source type
- direct fact, company claim, external evidence, or inference
- conflicts and caveats
- final wording and confidence
- implication for the decision

Use first-party sources for current product facts, pricing, policies, and positioning. Use independent research or customer evidence for efficacy, weaknesses, sentiment, and market behavior. Never use an unsourced AI statement as evidence.

## Research and Synthesize

Use a consistent unit of comparison. For competitive work, compare at least:

- customer job and target segment
- direct, secondary, or indirect alternative
- positioning and core promise
- sensing, insight, and intervention model
- price, recurring cost, and switching cost
- proof, trust, and adoption friction
- strength, weakness, and implication for the focal product

Do not give every competitor equal weight. Select a decision-relevant main set and keep weaker adjacencies as benchmarks or appendix material.

Turn observations into patterns, tensions, whitespace, and actions. Every major section must answer `so what`, `why now`, and `what should happen next`.

## Improve AI Outputs

Apply this loop to material outputs:

1. **Generate** a broad candidate set or first draft.
2. **Verify** factual claims against the source hierarchy.
3. **Challenge** category choices, causal claims, sample bias, and missing alternatives.
4. **Reclassify** facts and competitors when evidence contradicts the draft.
5. **Rewrite** in decision-ready language with calibrated confidence.
6. **Record** at least two meaningful before-and-after examples for the AI workflow note.

Use counter-hypotheses. Ask what evidence would make the recommendation wrong. Distinguish a company's marketing claim from independent validation, and never convert correlation, testimonials, or internal studies into proven causation.

## Create the Deliverable Stack

Keep artifacts consistent:

- Spreadsheet or research log: raw evidence, URLs, scoring, calculations, and claim ledger.
- Deck or report: answer-first narrative, selective evidence, recommendation, trade-offs, and next actions.
- Landing page or campaign asset: research-derived message and one clear action.
- Measurement plan: funnel events, segment dimensions, decision thresholds, guardrails, and learning loop.
- AI workflow note: tools used, verification method, concrete corrections, limitations, and human judgment.

Do not hide material caveats in an appendix. Do not overload the main deck with raw research.

## Quality Gates

Before delivery, confirm:

- Every material fact is attributable and current enough for its use.
- Prices and time-sensitive facts include an access date.
- Brand claims and independent evidence are visibly distinct.
- Competitors are classified by customer job, not superficial feature similarity.
- Strengths and weaknesses are evidenced or labeled as inference.
- Recommendations follow from findings and include trade-offs.
- Metrics have definitions, denominators, time windows, and decision use.
- The AI workflow shows concrete improvements, not a tool list.
- Slides, sheets, copy, and measurement use the same segments and positioning.
- The final output states uncertainty and what evidence should be collected next.

End with a clear recommendation, supporting evidence, important caveats, and the next action that most improves confidence.
